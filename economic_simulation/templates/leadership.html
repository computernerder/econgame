Manager duties and annual raise policy

A manager must be an active employee at work. Directors can cover management decisions within their own limit. Leaders cannot approve their own raises. Raises change future payroll; overdue reviews lower morale and can affect retention.

Appoint an employee director

Choose an existing employee. Appointment enables default growth policies and a shared authority budget; existing overrides remain. A manager appointed over their own business becomes a managing director, retaining one employment and payroll record. Subsidiaries inherit the nearest parent company’s director by default. An explicit assignment here overrides that default for this branch. Inherited businesses count toward the same workload and shared daily budget. Their existing employer pays the promotion raise: 15% on first director appointment, then 5% per additional business beyond the previously compensated scope. Limit-only edits and repeat assignments do not stack raises. The review shows the exact salary and payroll change. Each directly or indirectly covered business reserves one hour per working day from their normal role; maximum five businesses. The limit covers spending and annualized new base-pay commitments, shared across all their assigned businesses each day.
